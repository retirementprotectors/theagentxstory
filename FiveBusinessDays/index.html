The Six Acts

What actually happened, in order

Act I · Tuesday 08/11

The Promise, Not the Price

He signs the NDA at 8:23 AM and asks that everything route to his personal email — his assistant reads the work inbox, and the team does not know yet. That one request is the whole shape of the deal: a man protecting people on his way out the door.

“I’ve got enough money right now where I could retire. You know what I’m saying? It ain’t about that.”
GA Owner · discovery call, 16:06

The money question comes back empty. He has no after-tax target. What he has is a hometown where the local hospital sends people to him by name, and a season he refuses to work again.

“My clients are right here. I’m going to see them no matter which direction I go… I need them to be taken care of because I’m going to see them on a daily basis. That’s priority number one for me.”
GA Owner · discovery call, 09:59
“You sell somebody something, you’re selling them a promise. You’re going to take care of them forever. And that’s why it’s so difficult for people to retire in our business — they feel like they’re throwing their clients over.”
@JDM · discovery call, 09:43 · the reframe that ended the price conversation

The national buyers were already out — not on price, on terms of exit: they wanted him to stay three years. Ninety minutes in he prices it himself — “like 400 grand now and 600 in January. I’d take it today” — and names the real currency: “It’s about speed.”

Act II · Wednesday 08/12

He Hands Over the Keys

This is the act nobody writes down, and it decided the deal. He does not send spreadsheets. He gives up live administrator access to the CRM, the phone system and forty-odd carrier portals — relaying two-factor codes by text, one carrier at a time, between appointments and a golf league.

“I will set myself up as an Admin on these accounts for TONIGHT, in the event I get kicked out, so I don’t have to bug you or not finish on time. Once I get what I need, I’ll delete the User. That ok?”
@JDM · Tuesday night
“Yep”
GA Owner · one word, twelve hours after signing an NDA

Call recordings pulled and transcribed overnight. Books of business scraped carrier by carrier. Commission statements normalised. Clients, accounts, revenue, notes and recordings welded to a single identifier, end to end. Mid-sweep the fibre drops and takes four laptops, a server and a desktop with it. The work finishes on a phone hotspot.

“You’re the man”
GA Owner · Wednesday — the tone changes here and never changes back
Act III · Thursday 08/13

The Wobble, and the Competitor at the Door

Thursday is where the deal was actually at risk, and the risk was never price. A rival agency owner offers to get on a plane the next morning. He is being pulled by a buyer, a CPA, an attorney and 2,300 clients at once.

“Bro my nerves are shot. I’m getting hit from every direction. Agency owner from out of state is asking to come up tomorrow on a flight and check things out. Head spinning!”
GA Owner · Thursday

No counter-bid. He is handed a sentence to use on the competitor, and the two variables underneath the anxiety — the money and the clock — are removed instead.

“Just tell him you’ve gone into Due Diligence with a signed LOI, confidential Terms, and you’ll let him know ASAP if it gets sideways. … And we are LIQUID. So there’s no banks or anything else fucking around with this deal for you.”
@JDM, on RPI capital · Thursday
“I told him to chill man. We r good”
GA Owner · the competitor is gone in one message
Act IV · Friday 08/14

The Number Nobody Had to Take on Faith

One carrier has no book of business behind its portal at all. The question, put to the machine at 10:26 PM, refuses to guess: how many policies have produced revenue in 13 months, show active at the carrier, tie to an active client, and carry a policy type we have confirmed?

The honest answer is a range — 89 certain, 324 probable. Rather than take the flattering end, the discrepancy goes back to the seller to grade his own book. His field knowledge closes it. It is the 324 — a five-figure swing in his favour, on a line a buyer could have quietly rounded down.

“Jeez. The longer this takes and the more I audit, the more we’re paying you. I need to wrap this shit up before you own US!”
@JDM · Friday
“THE Answer is the one that is PROVABLE, regardless of who it benefits. We can talk about what to do about it, but a Fact is not an Opinion.”
@JDM · Friday · the sentence the whole valuation rests on

And then the part no spreadsheet buys. Twenty-seven years of his own work, rendered back to him as a picture.

“Makes me smile seeing in color the fruits of my hard labor… Very cool what you’ve done. Kind of emotional for me to look at it all.”
GA Owner · Friday
Act V · Friday 6:30 PM → Saturday 12:35 AM

Sixty Minutes to Signature

One hour on video with all three principals. The dashboard is walked live, the book line by line, and the offer lands inside the same hour: 1.25× on the forward run rate, ≈$1.175M — 40% ($470K) wired at closing, $705K as a 120-day deferred payment, secured by a promissory note with a personal guarantee.

“Is the 2nd payment contingent on anything what so ever is what I’m told to ask”
GA Owner · relaying his advisor
“NO. NO CONTINGENCIES. It’s not an earn-out or any other funny shit… I’ll have him sign a Personal Guarantee and a Promissory Note for you at Closing, to protect you too.”
@JDM · Friday
“Ya. I’m good w the LOI. Looks great to me.”
GA Owner · Friday evening · the green light

By 12:35 AM the returns, financials and contracts are in the shared folder, and the downline question is answered: everything moves with the assignment of revenue on the agency code — nothing changes for the agents underneath him. A seller who was “weighing his options” on Tuesday is talking about coming back to work by Friday night.

“I do feel less heaviness… Who knows what the future holds, but I can see myself doing something w you both in the future if I get bored.”
GA Owner · Friday night
Act VI · Tuesday 08/18, 8:00 AM — scheduled

The Wire

Counsel reviews over the weekend. Monday is the one day nobody is asked to work. Tuesday 8:00 AM the close is official — the full carrier forms kit executes in a single DocuSign, the agency code is assigned, and the upfront wire goes out.

From a signed NDA to funds in his account: five business days. The industry standard for the same transaction is a thirty-day close, and every large buyer he spoke to first wanted him to stay three years.

“There is no physical way on the planet that you can go end to end on a signed NDA in 5 business days on something like this.”
@JDM · Thursday · and then it was

Why It Closed

Four forces — and price is not one of them

It was won on four things you cannot get anywhere else at the same time — and if you are weighing offers right now, they are the four you should be pricing.
